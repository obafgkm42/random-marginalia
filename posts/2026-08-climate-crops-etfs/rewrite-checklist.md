# Rewrite checklist — 氣候、作物與農產品 ETF

- [x] Replace the vague title with one that names the article's actual subject: extreme climate, crop response, historical cases, 2026 conditions and ETF transmission.
- [x] Replace the snapshot-like structure with a recoverable causal line: climate system → crop biology → historical stress tests → 2026 conditions → supply and trade → investable instruments.

- [x] State the article's time boundary, geographic selection and evidentiary limits without repeating paragraph-final caveats.

## Climate explanation

- [x] Define weather, climate, drought, heat stress and flood or waterlogging where first needed.
- [x] Expand ENSO, NOAA, CPC and WMO at first mention; explain Niño-3.4, sea-surface-temperature anomaly and probabilistic seasonal outlooks.
- [x] Explain the Pacific mechanism: trade winds, thermocline and upwelling, Walker circulation, convection and teleconnections.
- [x] Explain why ENSO effects differ by season and region, and distinguish observed weather from attribution to ENSO.
- [x] Add the Indian monsoon and other regional controls where they materially affect the six crops.

## Crop biology and transmission

- [x] Wheat: planting and dormancy, heading or anthesis, grain filling, heat and drought timing, quality effects.
- [x] Corn: emergence, silking and pollination, kernel set and grain filling, heat and water stress.
- [x] Soybeans: flowering, pod set and seed filling, waterlogging, heat and crush-product transmission.
- [x] Rice: transplanting or direct seeding, flowering sterility, irrigation, flood and export-policy transmission.
- [x] Oil palm: perennial growth, sex determination and inflorescence development, fresh-fruit-bunch lag.
- [x] Sugar cane and beet: vegetative growth, sucrose accumulation, excess rain at harvest and cane-to-sugar-or-ethanol allocation.

## Historical record

- [x] Build a dated table of extreme-climate episodes, affected regions and crops, measured production or price effects, and evidentiary limitations.

- [x] Cover contrasting hazards rather than drought alone: heat, excessive rain or flood, and compound or sequential events.
- [x] Use historical cases to test mechanisms, not as a detached chronology or a claim that weather alone caused price changes.
- [x] Prefer primary government, intergovernmental or peer-reviewed sources; identify secondary reporting explicitly.
