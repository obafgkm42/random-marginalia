# Editorial and compliance checklist

This document is a practical publishing policy for Random Marginalia. It is not legal advice, and exceptions and limitations to copyright differ by jurisdiction. When a planned use is material, ambiguous or commercial, obtain advice for the relevant jurisdiction.

## 1. Keep a source record

For every material claim, record:

| Field | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Author or organisation, title and canonical URL |
| Access | Publication date or version, plus the date accessed |
| Evidence | Page or section and the claim used; retain an exact short excerpt only when necessary |
| Use | Paraphrase, direct quotation, adapted figure or dataset analysis |
| Rights | Stated licence, terms of use or permission; note when none is stated |
| Limits | Sample, methodology, date range, conflicts of interest and uncertainty |

Prefer the primary paper, dataset or report over a press release or a secondary summary. Archive enough bibliographic detail to recover a source if its URL changes, but do not republish the source itself.

## 2. Separate facts from protected expression

Individual facts and ideas may be used without copying the source's particular expression, but copyright rules, database rights and contract terms vary by jurisdiction. Therefore:

- write summaries independently after understanding the source;
- use a direct quotation only when the wording itself matters;
- keep quotations proportionate, clearly marked and attributed close to the text;
- do not recreate a report's distinctive chart, table, illustration or page layout merely by changing colours or labels; and
- do not assume that public access means permission to redistribute.

There is no universal "safe" word count or percentage for quotations. Context and substantiality matter more than a numeric threshold.

## 3. Mark third-party material item by item

The repository-wide CC BY 4.0 notice covers only rights held by this repository's author. If an article includes third-party material, add a notice next to that item containing:

1. the creator or publisher;
2. the source link;
3. the material's licence or the basis on which it is used; and
4. any required change notice.

Use wording such as:

> Adapted from *[title]* by [creator], [licence and link]. Changes: translated labels and simplified categories.

or, where permission or an exception is relied on:

> © [rightsholder]. Used with permission / used for quotation and commentary. Not included in this site's CC BY 4.0 licence.

Do not copy third-party charts, screenshots, report pages, logos, photographs or substantial passages unless the specific use has been reviewed against the source licence or permission.

## 4. Apply open licences precisely

- For CC-licensed material, preserve attribution, title, source, licence and modification information (TASL).
- Check the licence on the exact item or report; a website-wide licence may contain exceptions.
- Treat datasets separately from rendered survey pages. Database licences and download terms may impose attribution or share-alike duties.
- Keep third-party material outside this repository's own licence grant unless it is explicitly marked as compatible and sublicensable.
- Do not add restrictions to CC BY 4.0 material that would contradict the licence.

Useful references:

- [Creative Commons: marking third-party content](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Marking/Creators/Marking_third_party_content)
- [Creative Commons: considerations for licensors](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/licensing-considerations/version4/)
- [U.S. Copyright Office: copyright does not protect facts or ideas, but may protect their expression](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html)

These links are starting points, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific advice.

## 5. Handle privacy and confidentiality

Before publishing HTML, PDFs or repository history:

- remove personal data that is unnecessary for the article;
- do not publish customer data, private prompts, credentials, internal URLs or unpublished incident details;
- check employer, client and research-participant confidentiality obligations;
- inspect screenshots, filenames, HTML comments, PDF metadata and generated artefacts; and
- use aggregated or synthetic examples where individual records are not essential.

If an article discusses an employer's work, obtain any approval required by the applicable publication, invention-assignment or confidentiality policy.

## 6. Make product references neutral

Product and company names may identify the tools or studies discussed. Do not use logos as decoration or imply sponsorship, certification, affiliation or endorsement. Disclose vendor-funded research and commercial incentives close to the relevant claim.

Avoid unsupported statements about security, legality or performance. Describe the source, population, date and uncertainty instead of turning a limited study into a universal claim.

## 7. Disclose AI authorship accurately

State when AI tools materially researched or wrote an article. Do not claim a level of human review that did not occur. At minimum:

- name the AI tool when it is known;
- provide source links so readers can verify claims independently;
- say explicitly that errors may remain;
- distinguish automated drafting from any checks that actually occurred; and
- correct fabricated quotations, citations or product capabilities when discovered.

Do not rely on a model provider's output-ownership clause as proof that every generated passage is original or free of third-party rights.

## 8. Corrections and removal requests

Readers can open a GitHub issue with the article URL, disputed passage and supporting source. For privacy or confidentiality reports, include only the affected URL and request a private follow-up; do not repeat sensitive material in a public issue. For a credible privacy, confidentiality or rights complaint:

1. preserve the report privately;
2. temporarily remove the disputed material when continued publication creates meaningful risk;
3. check the source, licence and repository history;
4. correct, replace or restore the material with an explanatory note; and
5. regenerate the PDF so it matches the HTML.

Use Git history for editorial transparency, but remember that deleting a file in a later commit does not remove it from earlier commits. History rewriting may be required for secrets or sensitive personal data.

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Each material claim has a primary or clearly identified secondary source.
- [ ] Figures match the source's population, denominator, period and units.
- [ ] Findings, vendor claims and editorial interpretations are visibly distinct.
- [ ] Direct quotations are necessary, short, exact, marked and attributed.
- [ ] Every third-party image, chart, table or excerpt has a documented licence, permission or reviewed basis for use.
- [ ] Third-party items excluded from CC BY 4.0 are marked next to the item.
- [ ] Commercial interests, methodological limits and stale data are disclosed where relevant.
- [ ] HTML comments, links, screenshots, PDF metadata and Git diff contain no secrets or personal data.
- [ ] AI authorship and the actual level of human checking are described accurately.
- [ ] The article footer, source list, index entry and generated PDF agree.
- [ ] Links were checked and the article was reviewed on desktop, mobile and print layouts.
